# Audit the typed readout, then buy real documents

The cheapest large lever for Krino-2B is not a training run: under the last published JevBench method, a global temperature of T = 1.95 is applied to all three request types, and the v1.5 rules score a Noul answer with P(yes) between 0.20 and 0.80 as *wrong* and score a Score answer by its probability-weighted *expected level*, so one soft global T can be silently taxing two of the three equal-weighted thirds of Intelligence while buying Calibration points that the (I/50)² gate makes nearly worthless. That audit costs $0 and should precede every run below. After it, the evidence from the three public Kev-architecture logs (strands-decider, Kev, Malkuth) is unambiguous about what moves held-out hard-tier performance at 2B: **balanced answer-adequacy rows (strands v19: HelpSteer2 held-out 0.483→0.739, p<0.0001, Brier 0.388→0.342, $32)**, **real multi-step documents with a frozen Qwen3.5-4B teacher's distributions (strands v14: HotpotQA 0.705→0.750, p=0.0007, ~$2 of teacher time)**, and **verifier-filtered LLM-written workplace documents (strands v16: unseen-domain 0.646→0.837, p<0.0001, $130, and already committed for reuse at $0)** — all with replay toward the parent's own distributions rather than gold or a KL anchor (strands v17 restored MuSiQue 0.827→0.880 at zero new rows; Kev calls the base anchor "not a lever"). Everything else in the logs — templated synthesis, SuperNI volume, question transforms, calibration losses, reliability heads, checkpoint averaging, WiSE-FT, permutation averaging, option isolation, instruct torsos, longer context, a Gemma 4 E2B or MiniCPM5 torso — is null, negative, or costs the whole remaining budget for a measured −8.5-point hard-tier result (Jeff's same-recipe Gemma E2B 48.6 vs Qwen3.5-2B 57.1). Because v1.6.1 gates the composite by (I/50)², every Intelligence point at Krino's level is worth ≈1.3 composite points while maxing Calibration is worth 0.2; the plan below spends ≈$95 of the $123 Modal balance plus ≈$50–70 of OpenRouter credit on a $0 serving audit and three two-seed deltas (judge+documents, JevBench-shaped abstention, and a one-delta rebuild as the hedge against stacking erosion), each with a pre-registered kill criterion on the frozen development panel. The realistic target is Intelligence ≈23–28 (composite ≈10–17, the top of the 2B class, versus Decision 2B's 10.3 and Malkuth-2B's 4.0), not the 35–43 of the 4B rows, and the v1.6 method is unpublished, so the plan is built to hold under both the item-weighted and the macro-averaged-category readings.

## The (I/50)² gate makes Intelligence the only axis worth a dollar

The live board's formula is verifiable from its own rows: **Score = 4/(1/I + 1/C + 1/S + 1/K), multiplied by (axis/50)² for any of Intelligence, Speed or Cost below 50** ([benchmarkheaven v1.6.1](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models/v1.6.1)). Reproducing every published row to ±0.1 confirms the gate is live: Decision 2B's axes (I 22.9, C 86.8, S 90.0, K 66.4) give an ungated harmonic mean of 49.2 and a gated score of 10.3, exactly as displayed; Plumb-4B's I 43.0 gives 65.4 × 0.74 = 48.4 against a displayed 48.3 ([v1.6.1](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models/v1.6.1)). Holding Decision 2B's other three axes fixed, Intelligence 30 scores 20.3, 35 scores 29.6 and 40 scores 40.8, whereas raising Calibration from 86.8 to a perfect 100 adds 0.2, Speed 90→100 adds 0.15 and halving input tokens (Cost 66→75) adds 0.3. One Intelligence point at I ≈ 23 is worth ≈1.3 composite points; the whole Calibration axis is worth less than one. That asymmetry decides every trade-off in this report, including the calibration ones: when a serving choice trades Calibration for typed competence, Intelligence wins.

Where Krino-2B sits today can only be interpolated, because the board publishes no per-tier figures at v1.6.1. Its public read is **161/231 (69.7 %), hard 47/111 (42.3 %), hard-tier Brier 0.706 and ECE 0.236 at T = 1.95**, with hard-v1 dev accuracy 0.717 and served ECE 0.041 ([krino RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md)). Malkuth-2B reads 159/231 on the same harness and carries I 15.9 (composite 4.0) at v1.6.1; decider-2b read 164/231 (0.710) at v1.4.2.2 and carries I 20.7 (7.7); Decision 2B read 174/231 and carries I 22.9 (10.3) ([v1.6.1](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models/v1.6.1); [results JSON v1.4.2.2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/results/v1.4.2.2/jevbench-v1.4.2.2-results.json)). By that ordering Krino would land near I 16–20 and a composite of 4–8 — a number that doubles at I 23 and quadruples at I 28. The entire collapse of the 2B rows between v1.5.1 and v1.6.1 (Decision 2B 58.8→10.3, Malkuth-2B 55.4→4.0) is explained by Intelligence alone: the inventory moved to **300 open + 1,200 sealed = 1,500 decisions (80 % sealed)**, and the category mix moved to **Rules, policy & law 763 (51 %), Coding & software 302 (20 %), Math & numbers 193 (13 %), Support & operations 93, Finance & commerce 66, Everyday language 46, Safety & security 37** — a reversal from v1.5.1, where Support (282) and Finance (224) were large and Coding tiny (92) ([v1.6.1](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models/v1.6.1); [v1.5.1](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models/v1.5.1)). The categories where small models score best shrank from 24 % to 9 % of items; the two that map onto the hard families that break 2B models (long_policy, multi_hop, temporal_numeric, probability → law and math) grew to 64 %.

What the Intelligence axis *is* remains the last published definition, the frozen v1.5 method plus its 26 September headline amendment, because no v1.6 method document, release note, results JSON or scoring module is reachable (all candidate paths return 404) ([METHOD-v1.5.md](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5.md)). Under v1.5, competence is chance-corrected per request type and per tier with the chance baseline computed per item: **Choice CC = 100·(acc − c̄)/(1 − c̄)** with c̄ the mean of 1/K; **Noul: P(yes) ≤ 0.20 is No, ≥ 0.80 is Yes, "anything in between is an abstention, counted wrong"**; **Score: CC = 100·(1 − mean nMAE/mean nMAE_chance), where "the prediction is the expected position if a distribution is returned"**; tiers are weighted easy 0.10 / standard 0.20 / judge 0.30 / hard 0.40 and the three types are combined **one third each** ([METHOD-v1.5.md §3](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5.md); [headline addendum](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5-ADDENDUM-HEADLINE-A-EQUAL-TYPES.md)). On a 4-option hard item, Krino's 42.3 % is CC 23; Decision 2B's 58.2 % (220 items) is CC 44; 50 % would be CC 33. The Speed and Cost formulas reproduce v1.6.1's published axes exactly (100 − 30·log10(13) = 66.6 ≈ Decision 2B's 66.4), which is the best available evidence that the v1.5 Intelligence rules also survived into v1.6 unchanged.

The assignment's premise that v1.6.1 Intelligence is a macro average over the seven subject categories with a ≥30-item spoke rule could not be confirmed in any primary or secondary source; the categories and their counts are confirmed, how they enter the score is not ([v1.6.1](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models/v1.6.1)). This matters for allocation: under item weighting, law and coding are 71 % of Intelligence and the small categories are worth 5 % together; under a macro average, Everyday language (46 items) and Safety & security (37) would each weigh as much as law, making them the cheapest places to gain. The plan handles this by targeting the families that dominate under item weighting (long_policy, multi_hop, tradeoff, judge_hard, ambiguous → Rules/policy/law; judge arithmetic → Math; devtools → Coding) while relying on the breadth mix already in Krino (MASSIVE, CLINC150, Aegis 2.0, jailbreak-classification) to keep the small categories off chance, and by adding one $0 report-only read: `datasets/topics.json` labels the topic of each of the 231 public items ([TOPICS.md](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/datasets/TOPICS.md)), so a per-category public summary can show whether any category sits at chance without reading per-item results, which EVAL.md forbids. Two further rules shape the plan: the v1.5 overfit penalty bites only when a system's open→sealed gap exceeds the field median by more than 8 CC points, so a system that never trains on public items (Krino's rule) is safe, and a label-only output scores Calibration 0, which under the harmonic mean zeroes the composite — Krino must keep serving native distributions for all three types ([METHOD-v1.5.md §3.4, §4](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5.md)).

## A $0 readout audit may out-earn every training run

Krino's T = 1.95 was fitted on the nine krino-breadth development suites, where it is ≈65 % accurate, and gives hard-v1 dev ECE 0.041 but public hard-tier ECE 0.236 at 42 % accuracy ([krino EVAL.md](../EVAL.md)). The over-confidence on the hard tier is the gap between mean confidence and 42 %, a distribution-shift problem every log on this architecture reports: Kev's finding 5 states that a temperature fitted on easy in-distribution rows serves hard-v1 at ECE 0.137 (4B) against 0.067 with a temperature refit on hard-v1's own held-out rows, and that **a single global T transfers across workloads while "per-(type, K) temperatures and a logistic reliability head made things worse"** ([Kev PLAN.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md)). The only within-system evidence that a temperature change transfers to sealed items is the NInfer 27B pair, where moving T from 1 to 1.5 took sealed ECE from 0.299 to 0.202 at identical accuracy ([results JSON v1.4.2.2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/results/v1.4.2.2/jevbench-v1.4.2.2-results.json)). But the strands "calmix" refit is the warning: a temperature sharpened on a mixed pool improved the standard tier and *worsened* hard-tier Brier 0.581→0.632, because "the answers v19 gets right and the answers it gets wrong need opposite corrections, and one scalar moves both" ([PREREGISTRATION-v19-calmix.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/preregistrations/PREREGISTRATION-v19-calmix.md)). Taking ECE from 0.236 to 0.15 is worth about +4 Calibration points on the ECE half of the axis, which the gate turns into roughly +0.2 composite — not nothing, but not where the money is.

The money is in the interaction nobody in the logs discusses, because all three were written under v1.2–v1.4 rules. Under v1.5's Noul rule, a yes/no answer counts only when P(yes) leaves the (0.20, 0.80) band; with a two-option softmax at T = 1.95 that requires a raw logit margin of **1.95 × ln 4 ≈ 2.7 nats**, against 1.4 nats at T = 1.0. Under v1.5's Score rule a distribution is scored by its expected level, so softening pulls every prediction toward the middle of the scale: for a 5-level item whose argmax is correct, a logit vector of (3.0, 1.5, 0.5, 0, −0.5) gives expected position 0.47 and CC 76 at T = 1.0 but expected position 1.10 and CC 45 at T = 1.95 (my arithmetic from the published formula). Strands measured the same mechanism on its own data: "Calibrating `score` costs accuracy … held-out score MAE degraded from 0.847 to 0.981" ([strands history.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/history.md)). Since Noul and Score are each one third of Intelligence and Intelligence is gated, a global T chosen for Choice ECE can cost more composite than it earns. Whether it does for Krino is unmeasured and free to measure: Kev's benchmark writes per-row probabilities, so the typed CC of hard-v1 dev, documents-v1 dev and devtools-v1 dev can be recomputed under the v1.5 rules at T ∈ {1.0, 1.3, 1.95} per type on CPU, together with the share of Noul answers inside the band and the Score nMAE. The decision rule that follows from the gate is per-type: Choice keeps the ECE-optimal temperature (argmax is invariant), while Noul and Score take the temperature that maximises typed competence on held-out development rows, with their ECE reported. This is exactly the one place where the strands and Kev disagreement about per-type temperatures resolves — strands' per-primitive gain (ECE 0.106→0.037) came from fitting against ECE rather than NLL and from `score` needing a saturated T = 6.00 while choice/noul sat at 1.40/1.13 ([strands history.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/history.md)), and Kev's loss came from fitting per-(type, K) for ECE out of distribution; neither tested per-type T against a competence rule. Hopper (choice 0.790 / noul 0.753 / score 0.900), decider-2b and Manchego all ship per-type maps, so the format is accepted by the board ([Hopper card](https://huggingface.co/HopitAI/hopper)).

Two more $0 checks belong in the same pass. The Choice temperature should be refit on a pool at hard-tier difficulty that Krino never trained on — hard-v1 dev is now in-distribution because hard-v1 train ×2 is in the mix — using Kev's `calibrate_checkpoint.py`, which refuses in-distribution rows by design and never changes argmax ([calibrate_checkpoint.py](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/scripts/calibrate_checkpoint.py)); candidate pools are HotpotQA (never trained, 959 questions) and the low-accuracy breadth suites, with hard-v1 dev ECE as the before/after guard, and with the caveat that the public-hard ECE estimate on 111 items carries a sampling sd of roughly 0.03–0.04 (Kev measured 0.006–0.010 on ~2,000-question panels), so only a change ≥0.05 is readable ([Kev PLAN.md round 28](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md)). And the serving path should be checked for the strands tokenisation bug rather than for context length: Krino's `max_state 7552` already exceeds the hard tier's 2,000–6,000-token long_policy spec, strands found the context curve "flat past ~3072" (1024/3072/4096 → 0.632/0.636/0.636, +1 item at p = 1.000), and the fix that mattered was reserving the question's tokens first after a bug "left 8 tokens for the question" — "The model was picking among options it could not see" — which recovered ECE 0.115→0.079 ([strands jevbench.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/evaluation/jevbench.md); [HARD-TIER.md](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/datasets/HARD-TIER.md)). Over-long states must truncate (`KEV_TRUNCATE_STATES=1`) rather than 422, because a refusal scores as wrong, and Kev-0.8B's own validated context is 8,192 because it loses 5.2 pp [−8.5, −2.1] at 16k ([Kev README](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/README.md); [Kev PLAN.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md)).

Permutation averaging and training-time calibration losses are settled negatives at this size. Kev's 16-rotation averaging removed order flips (0.083→0.028 at 4B) but moved accuracy −0.3/+0.2 pp with intervals spanning zero and multiplied median latency 2.2–3.0× ([Kev archived PLAN.md 4.4](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/research-archive-2026-09-24/PLAN.md)); at a 2B raw p50 of 0.045 s, K serial passes under the ×2 + 0.15 s rule cost 4–9 Speed points (≈ −0.5 composite) for no Intelligence gain, and Krino's flip rate already fell from 13.9 % to 2.8 % through training-time shuffling alone ([krino RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md)). Kev's round-3 screen of label smoothing, CE + Brier and focal loss against a matched CE control found no candidate — smoothing destroyed selective coverage (0.576→0.006 at ≤5 % error) and focal improved ECE 0.052→0.043 without improving ranking ([Kev archived PLAN.md round 3](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/research-archive-2026-09-24/PLAN.md)). The one training-time calibration change with intervals excluding zero is soft targets on genuinely ambiguous items (Kev-9B Brier −0.024 [−0.036, −0.013] versus a matched hard-label delta, fitted T falling to ≈1.0), with the caveat that softened MNLI targets caused a WANLI dip, so it belongs only on sources that are ambiguous by construction ([Kev archived PLAN.md 4.9](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/research-archive-2026-09-24/PLAN.md)).

Three patterns explain the ranking. First, every gain came from data that is *structurally diverse and either human-labelled or verifier-filtered* — ContractNLI's 17 fixed hypotheses over 607 real NDAs, HelpSteer2's human ratings, 1,000 LLM-written documents across 34 domains kept only when two independent verifier answers matched the writer's — while every failure was single-structure data that reached ceiling on its own generator and left JevBench untouched: RuleTaker (held-out depth-3 +0.173, JevBench "identical to four decimals"), 20,000 generated rule-execution documents (synthetic eval 0.436→0.972, JevBench 0.658→0.658), 20,000 minimal pairs (0.628, multi_hop 7→4/18), ShARC/ConditionalQA (MuSiQue −0.024, p=0.0017) ([strands generations.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/generations.md)). Kev's version of the same lesson is diminishing returns on one generator — "+26 pp for the first 6,000 hard-v1 records, +5 for the next 12,000" — and Krino already carries hard-v1 train twice, so more of it is the one thing the hard-tier evidence argues *against* ([Kev PLAN.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md)). Second, teacher distributions help only where the teacher is actually better: a frozen Qwen3.5-4B reads 0.805 on the 231 public items against the frozen 2B's 0.658, and its distributions on long multi-step rows transferred (v14), but on short classification rows the same teacher lowered held-out NLL while raising JevBench Brier 0.342→0.355 "from a few confident wrong answers" (v20), and v12's short-task distillation lost 8 public tasks (p=0.096) ([strands history.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/history.md)). The parent's own distributions beat the frozen 4B's as replay targets on identical rows (v16 vs v17), and no log has tested parent-distribution replay on short rows — Krino's next delta is the natural place. Third, the yes-bias fix is the one data change that moved calibration across the whole benchmark: every strands model "answered 'adequate' to 11 of its 12 public tasks" before v19, and JevBench's hard tier is authored with Noul golds roughly half yes ([HARD-TIER.md](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/datasets/HARD-TIER.md)); Krino's own yes-rate on hard Noul items against the 47 % gold rate is a one-line diagnostic that should precede any generation spend.

The abstention question deserves a direct answer because chance correction changes its sign. An abstention — no label, or a Noul probability in the band — is strictly worse than a guess, because it counts as wrong while a guess has 1/K chance; the scorer states "An abstention (label None) is a valid-but-empty answer that counts wrong" ([scoring.py](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/jevbench/scoring.py)). The only abstention worth training is choosing an explicit `insufficient_information`-style label when it is gold, which the `ambiguous` family is built to reward "about half" the time, mixed with cases that "look ambiguous yet are decided by one overlooked fact" ([HARD-TIER.md](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/datasets/HARD-TIER.md)). The Kev lineage's none-pairs (`p_none_pair 0.25`, kept because "they teach 'none of the above'") teach rejection of all options, not that pattern, and the Kev-lineage rows (kev 4B, kev 8B, Malkuth-2B, Malkuth-4B) score **0.108–0.135 on the sealed ambiguous_abstain family against Plumb-4B's 0.595 and Jev's 0.297** ([results JSON v1.4.2.2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/results/v1.4.2.2/jevbench-v1.4.2.2-results.json); [Kev PLAN.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md)). That is the single largest per-item headroom on the sealed set for a Kev-architecture model, and the strands v20 result tells you how to build the data without paying its tax: the authors conclude "the catch-all rows need the distractor case weighted more heavily than the absent case, not equally" ([PREREGISTRATION-v20.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/preregistrations/PREREGISTRATION-v20.md)). JevBench-shaped abstain items — a named abstain label with a crisp criterion, look-alike decided cases up-weighted, soft targets where the verifiers disagree — are untested in any log, which is why they sit at rank 7 as a conditional run rather than at the top.

## What not to buy, and why the torso stays Qwen3.5-2B

The explicit negative list is long enough that the cheapest way to lose the remaining budget is to re-run one of its entries. Krino's own PLAN schedules a KL-anchor ablation next; the evidence says skip it. Kev's anchoring toward the frozen base's zero-shot distribution at weights 0.3–1.0 moved knowledge-MCQ transfer +2.6 [−1.8, +6.8] at best and left MMLU and PAWS unchanged — "Not a lever" — and WiSE-FT interpolation toward the base at α 0.50 cost 4.4 pp without restoring the lost suite ([Kev archived PLAN.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/research-archive-2026-09-24/PLAN.md); [Kev PLAN.md round 20](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md)); strands' KL-to-frozen arm added one item (p=1.000) and its KL on changed questions lost ten (p=0.013) because the frozen torso "answers yes on 60 of 74 yes/no tasks" and is "not a teacher for yes/no" ([strands history.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/history.md)). Replay toward the trained parent is the forgetting remedy with evidence; the base anchor is not. Likewise not worth a dollar: more same-generator hard-v1 rows (Kev round 14, +3.6 to +4.0 with the ECE guard failing), stacking a second delta at 0.8B (round 13, "Stacking a second delta on the 0.8B erodes the first" — the risk the plan's hedge exists for), per-(type, K) temperatures and a logistic reliability head fitted for ECE (AURC worse by +0.006 to +0.021 at 4B), probability-averaged seed ensembles (4B Brier +0.011 [+0.003, +0.018] worse), checkpoint averaging, 9B→0.8B self-distillation, question-side LoRA (−3.4 to −6.0 pp), option isolation (exact permutation invariance but −5.8 pp at 4B), `perm_kl`, two epochs on a delta, full-weight SFT instead of LoRA on the same data (round 19: "cost short states −3.0 and scienthoon −3.7 and gained nothing"), and instruct torsos (strands v6/v8: `policy` fell "precisely where the frozen base sits", `routing_hard` −0.400) ([Kev PLAN.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/PLAN.md); [strands history.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/history.md)). On the data side, CUAD's 41 clause types dragged Malkuth's held-out LEDGAR from 0.65 to 0.59 and uniform toxicity sampling taught +0.35 levels of over-rating, so Krino's exclusion of CUAD stands and LEDGAR is the clause source if one is wanted ([build_train_mix.py](https://github.com/newfull5/malkuth/blob/main/tools/build_train_mix.py)). Temporal/numeric reasoning is the family to stop chasing with data at this size: no non-reasoning system passes 0.533 on it, Jev scores 0.27, the frozen Qwen3.5-4B 3 of 15, and three strands attempts left it at 2 of 15 ([strands jevbench.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/evaluation/jevbench.md)).

The torso question has one controlled measurement, and it points away from Gemma. Jeff trained the same AutoJev-style recipe on three bases on one RTX PRO 6000: **Qwen3.5-2B untrained 45.7 → trained 57.1 on the hard tier; Gemma 4 E2B untrained 41.0 → trained 48.6** ([Jeff-Gemma4-E2B card](https://huggingface.co/mstrasser/Jeff-Gemma4-E2B)), with the caveat that the Gemma variant is a v1.0 build limited to 26 options while the Qwen variants are v1.1, so the data were not perfectly matched — but the untrained gap points the same way. On the board, the only Gemma 4 E2B entry (system-one-open) sits at hard 0.491 in the same band as the Qwen3.5-2B rows (decider-2b 0.473, Malkuth-2B 0.445), and every strong Gemma-4 result is a 12B or 31B torso ([results JSON v1.4.2.2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/results/v1.4.2.2/jevbench-v1.4.2.2-results.json)). E2B is "2.3B effective, 5.1B total including embeddings" — it does not meet a literal ≤2B target and stores 9.3 GB in Jeff's packaging against 4.2 GB for Qwen3.5-2B — and although Gemma 4 shipped under Apache-2.0 on 2 April 2026, the board records Gemma rows "with Google's Gemma Prohibited Use Policy" and declined to run a gated Gemma base because "We do not accept binding terms on Florian's behalf" ([google/gemma-4-E2B](https://huggingface.co/google/gemma-4-E2B); [Google OSS blog](https://opensource.googleblog.com/2026/03/gemma-4-expanding-the-gemmaverse-with-apache-20.html); [RESULTS.md](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/RESULTS.md)). Kev's packed block-causal mask would actually favour an attention-only torso, but nobody has run Kev on Gemma 4's 512-token sliding-window and KV-shared layers, so an A/B carries engineering time before the first step; a stage-1 A/B is cheap (≈$12–19 for two torsos × two seeds) but tells you nothing the full mix would not reverse, and a full-mix A/B is **≈$84–197**, the whole remaining budget, to confirm a result Jeff already measured as negative ([Kev AGENTS.md](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/AGENTS.md)). MiniCPM5-2B carries the best 2B-class hard score on the board (Decision 2B 0.582, ECE 0.080) but is 2.52B dense with 42 layers, its best derivative is "an evaluation preview … not a cleared commercial release", and its pinned stack (transformers 4.57.6) predates Kev's `transformers>=5.17` requirement ([openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B](https://huggingface.co/openbmb/MiniCPM5-2B); [results JSON v1.4.2.2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/results/v1.4.2.2/jevbench-v1.4.2.2-results.json)). The only torso lever with a measured effect inside the 2B class is generation (Qwen3→Qwen3.5 at 2B, +0.013), and the levers with large effects are size (frozen Qwen3.5-2B 0.658 → 4B 0.805; 1.7B→4B +0.043 trained) — outside the class ([strands history.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/research/history.md)). Board classing adds nothing to the decision: there is no parameter bucket, only the Jev-class cost/latency band (≤ $0.065 per 1,000 decisions, adjusted p50 ≤ 1.23 s), and a 2B adapter is priced at its base's hosted reference regardless of torso ([benchmarkheaven live](https://benchmarkheaven.com/jev-models); [pricing addendum](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5-ADDENDUM-PRICING.md)). Krino stays on Qwen3.5-2B-Base, Apache-2.0, 1.9B.

Serving is engineering, not research, and the headroom is small but real. A 2B pointer head on an L4/L40S lands at Speed 90–95 whatever the GPU, because the +0.15 s floor caps self-hosted Speed near 96.5; what blows the Jev-class band is a bad serving path (Open-Jev 2B's 0.665 s raw p50 with prefix caching off scored Speed 73.5) ([results JSON v1.4.2.2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/results/v1.4.2.2/jevbench-v1.4.2.2-results.json)). Cost is floored at the base model's OpenRouter/DeepInfra list price times measured input tokens with zero output for a readout backend, so the prompt template is the only lever: Decision 2B, measured at 269 input tokens per decision at v1.4.2.2, scores 66.4 on $0.013 per 1,000 decisions at v1.6.1; halving a 560-token template is worth ≈9 Cost points, ≈0.3 composite ([METHOD-v1.5-ADDENDUM-PRICING-INTERPRETATION-1](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5-ADDENDUM-PRICING-INTERPRETATION-1.md)). Batching is explicitly unscored.

## The next four runs, costed, with the read that confirms or kills each

The plan honours Krino's frozen rules — two seeds per candidate, checkpoints chosen on the development panel, public items report-only — and EVAL.md's decision clause: a gain of hard-v1 dev +2.0 pp or transfer-v4 +1.5 pp or breadth mean +1.5 pp with both seeds in the same direction, decision-v7 and transfer-v4 within −1.0 pp, hard-v1 served ECE up by no more than 0.02, transfer-v4 flip rate up by no more than 2 pp ([krino EVAL.md](../EVAL.md)). Two practical constraints bind: Modal's workspace cap is $100/month and must be raised before the first run, and H100 hosts varied 0.113–0.173 s/record on identical configs, so trials get a 6 h ceiling and the rebuild in Run 3 should be split or moved to an H200 ($4.54/h), which Kev found faster on its documents stage ([krino PLAN.md](../../PLAN.md); [kev-4b card](https://github.com/jaredpalmer/kev/blob/main/docs/model-cards/kev-4b.md)). Every new training file is screened with `screen_overlap.py` before use, and strands' committed files are re-screened at 8-gram because strands did not state that screen.

Modal spend across the plan is ≈$70–95 against the $123 balance, plus ≈$50–70 of OpenRouter credit for Run 2, inside the few-hundred-dollar ceiling with ≈$30–50 of Modal headroom for a retry on a slow host. The ordering follows the gain-per-dollar table: Run 0 can move two thirds of Intelligence for nothing and must be read before any training, because a per-type map changes which checkpoint looks best; Run 1 bundles the three p < 0.01 data levers into one delta rather than three, both because Kev's lesson at 0.8B is to "train deltas together, not stacked" and because three two-seed runs would cost $60 for the same rows ([krino LESSONS.md](../LESSONS.md)). Run 1's HotpotQA read is the critical one: v16's documents cost HotpotQA 0.038 (p=0.002) and v17's parent-replay restored the replayed sources but not HotpotQA, so a flat or rising HotpotQA with the parent-replay in place is the first evidence that parent-distribution replay protects transfer and not only the rows replayed. Run 2 is the only purchase of new generation, aimed at the one sealed family where the Kev lineage sits at 0.108 and the leaders at 0.3–0.6; its 300-item held-out split also gives Run 0's Choice temperature the hard-difficulty, never-trained pool it currently lacks. Run 3 exists because the strongest counter-evidence to Run 1's design is Kev's round 13, and because Krino's stage 2 already succeeded as a single combined delta from stage 1 — rebuilding is the known-good shape if stacking erodes.

Reads must be sized to what the instruments resolve. The 231 public items cannot resolve a difference below about +0.043 (ten tasks), one retrain moves the public count by about 3 tasks (six v17 retrains: 159–167, SD 3.2), and two single runs differ by about 4.5, so every public number in this plan is report-only and every decision is made on the development panel, where hard-v1 dev has 1,083 questions, documents-v1 920, devtools-v1 1,074 and transfer-v4 656 (±1.7 pp) ([strands results.md](https://github.com/strands-labs/strands-decider/blob/main/evaluation/results.md); [krino RESULTS.md](../RESULTS.md)). The board's own resolution is similar: the v1.5 design puts the 95 % half-width of an Intelligence difference at ±2.8 and a single-system SE near 1.6, so two Intelligence points between Krino and Decision 2B will not separate on the board either ([METHOD-v1.5.md §2](https://github.com/fstandhartinger/jevbench/blob/main/docs/METHOD-v1.5.md)). What will separate is the gate: the difference between I 17 and I 25 is a composite of 4.8 versus 12.9.

## Conclusion

The decisive finding is that JevBench's typed scoring and its (I/50)² gate together invert the usual calibration instinct for a 2B model: the softer the global temperature, the better the hard-tier ECE and the worse the Noul and Score thirds of a gated Intelligence axis, so the first job is to measure what T = 1.95 costs under the v1.5 rules and serve each request type at the temperature its own rule rewards. That audit, the parent-distribution replay, the reuse of strands' committed verified data and the HelpSteer2 conversion are all free or nearly so, and they exhaust the interventions with p < 0.01 held-out evidence at this architecture; the only purchase with a plausible but unproven return is JevBench-shaped abstention data for the one sealed family where every Kev-lineage model sits near 0.1. Everything in the long negative list — including the KL anchor the project had queued next and any torso swap — should be treated as already paid for by someone else.

Two uncertainties set the ceiling and the risk. The v1.6 method is unpublished, so the plan assumes v1.5's typed, per-item chance-corrected rules survived (the Speed and Cost axes say they did) and hedges category weighting with the breadth already in the mix and a $0 per-category public read; if v1.6.1 turns out to macro-average categories, the 83 Everyday and Safety items become the cheapest Intelligence on the board and the breadth sources deserve a second look. And the 2B torso's ceiling on temporal/numeric and long-policy reading is real — no 2B row has exceeded sealed Intelligence ≈28 on a same-difficulty sealed set — so the honest goal of these runs is the top of the 2B class at a composite of 10–17, with the 4B tier reachable only by leaving the class.
